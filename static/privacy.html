ShardGate

Privacy policy

Last updated November 15, 2026. Plain language, grounded in how ShardGate actually works.

What ShardGate stores about you

An account record: your email address, a one-way bcrypt hash of your password (the password itself is never stored), and the account creation time. That record lives in the site's SQLite database.

Your documents

Every document you add is encrypted with AES-256-GCM before it is written to disk. The master key never exists whole on disk: it is split into 3 Shamir shares with a 2-of-3 threshold, and the shares are reassembled in memory only for the instant a decrypt needs them, then wiped. Ciphertext, shares and an append-only audit log are stored per account in an isolated vault directory.

Sessions

Signing in sets one session cookie. It is HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict, and holds a signed token that expires after 7 days. No trackers, no analytics, no third-party requests.

What the service does not do

ShardGate does not sell data, embed ads, or send your documents anywhere. The default answerer is offline and extractive: answers are built from your own decrypted text inside the server's memory. If a remote-LLM adapter is ever enabled, only the text chunks needed to answer your question would be sent to that provider; it is disabled by default.

Your controls

GET /api/auth/export returns a full copy of your data, including your documents in decrypted form. DELETE /api/auth/me deletes your account record and your entire vault directory permanently. Signing out clears the session cookie.

Rate limits and lockout

Register and login endpoints are rate-limited, and 5 failed logins from one address and account trigger a temporary lockout. These counters live in memory only.

Limits of this deployment

On free hosting the disk is ephemeral: a redeploy or restart resets the database and vaults, and accounts created before it are gone. Nothing is backed up elsewhere.
